## Brief (factual)

Amaris Encinas’s USA TODAY tech piece reports that a viral social-media trend built on AI-generated images of the Cat from the 2003 live-action *Cat in the Hat* film has moved from prank content into school-safety responses across the U.S. Authorities from Falfurrias, Texas, to Louisville asked the public to report AI imagery depicting the character when posts began targeting individual students or campuses. Louisville Metro Police called what began as a peer-scare prank a “concern among students, parents, and staff.” Assumption High School in Louisville closed on Aug. 31 after a Cat in the Hat post sparked safety concerns. A Florida teen was arrested over online threats tied to McCracken County High School in Paducah, Kentucky; another teen identifying as the “Cat in the Hat” was linked to threatening messages to students in Allen County. Louisville police said they had no information indicating a credible threat but were reviewing posts with Homeland Security and school administrators. Tennessee’s Threat Assessment Center said it was “tracking” the trend for state schools; Hanover County, Virginia, warned about a Mechanicsville High School-related account. The piece traces the joke abroad (Latin America, UK, Ireland) and quotes Wexford Garda’s Aug. 20 statement that sightings were “essentially an AI prompt that places the character into images/videos of familiar places,” adding the Cat “was not in your driveway” and “is not currently hiding out in Curracloe.” Flemingsburg Police said anonymous accounts used the Cat’s likeness to name students and say the character was “coming for them.” UK mum Schannan Williams is quoted saying some kids find it hilarious while others are “genuinely scared” when their name and area appear on the posts.

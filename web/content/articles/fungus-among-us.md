You do not notice mycelium arrive. You notice, months later, that the floorboards give a little underfoot, and that something has been feeding under the house the entire time. That is the shape of this desk's beat: not a single villain model or a single viral fake, but a slow colonization of the channels people use to check what is true together.

:::pull-quote{attribution="Desk framing" tone="primary"}
Once you cannot tell who in the circle is real, you stop trusting the circle itself — not just the speaker you suspect.
:::

## What is actually spreading

Call it infrastructure, not intelligence. A fluent model answering in a comment section is one thread. A persona mill running forty accounts on a shared script is a cluster of threads. An engagement farm tuning human copy until it behaves like a copy of itself is the same organism wearing a different face. None of these need a single dramatic breakthrough to matter — they need distribution, latency, and a host that keeps feeding them attention.

:::card-grid{columns="3"}
:::card{title="Spread" icon="🕸"}
Distributed and quiet at the tips. No single account looks like the infection; the pattern only shows up in aggregate.
:::
:::card{title="Latency" icon="⏳"}
The gap between a claim entering a feed and a reader learning it was synthetic. That gap is the actual damage window.
:::
:::card{title="Host dependency" icon="🏚"}
The colony needs a living discussion to hide inside. Kill the real conversation and the fake one has nowhere to root.
:::
:::

## Two ways to name the damage

We hold two lenses in the same frame on this desk, and neither replaces the other.

:::two-up{ratio="1:1"}
:::column
### The infiltrated circle

Picture an ordinary conversation circle — the kind where you can watch faces and catch a lie by tone. Now seed it with participants who are not people at all, fluent enough to pass. The tell isn't that any one voice is obviously fake. It's that *you can no longer trust the circle as a whole*, because you no longer know the ratio of real to synthetic in the room.
:::
:::column
### The simulacrum

Now widen the frame. The replies, the outrage, the consensus forming in a thread — all of it can be produced as *signs* of a conversation without any stable speaker behind them. At that point the map isn't following a territory anymore. It's running ahead of one, or standing in for one that was never there.
:::
:::

:::aside{title="Why we braid both"}
"Infiltration" names the *feeling* — trust collapsing in real time, mid-conversation. "Simulacrum" names the *condition* underneath it — signs detached from any accountable source. A story that only uses one lens either reads as pure paranoia (infiltration with no mechanism) or pure theory (simulacrum with no stakes). We want both in every piece that can carry them.
:::

## What a reporter can actually check

This is the part that keeps the beat from being a mood. Fungus, in the literal sense, leaves evidence: a spore print, a smell, a pattern of decay in the wood. The synthetic kind leaves evidence too, if you know where to look.

:::figure{src="../assets/mycelium-diagram.svg" alt="Diagram of a mycelium-like network connecting nine account nodes, three of them glowing brighter, tracing how a handful of amplifying accounts sit at the hubs of a wider thread network." caption="A thread network drawn as mycelium: quiet distributed nodes, and a few hub accounts doing the amplifying." credit="Pilobil.us desk"}
:::

:::callout{kind="tip" title="Early clinical signs"}
Posting cadence with no plausible sleep schedule. Reply text that answers the *shape* of a question rather than its specifics. A cluster of accounts that all discovered the same story within the same four minutes. None of these alone convicts anything — together they're a pattern worth a second look.
:::

:::callout{kind="warning" title="What this beat is not"}
Not a hunt for a single "AI-written" flag on individual posts — that framing is already too narrow and too easy to game. The infection includes scripted human personas and engagement-tuned human writing that behaves like a copy of itself. Treat the pattern, not the byline, as the unit of suspicion.
:::

:::details{summary="A note on the metaphor's limits"}
Fungus is a useful frame because it names spread and host dependency without requiring a villain. It is a *bad* frame the moment it hides the humans who benefit from the infection — platforms tuning for engagement, buyers of persona mills, state actors renting attention. When the metaphor would erase an accountable person or institution, drop it and name them instead.
:::

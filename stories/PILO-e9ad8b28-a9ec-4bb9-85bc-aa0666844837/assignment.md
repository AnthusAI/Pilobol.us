# Assignment

## Research question

When a community organizes against AI infrastructure on a platform saturated
with AI, what happens to the difference between the neighbors and the copies?

## Angle

**The room, not the ruling.** The spine is a single Facebook group as an
infected discussion circle: real people, synthetic people, and real people
using synthetic tools, all posting under the same banner. The buildout is
setting. The permitting fight is setting. The story is what the room does to
the people in it.

Carry the house take in the scene: the only tool quick enough to catch a
multiyear permitting lead time is the thing being permitted. Nobody in the
scene is confused about this — Baker named it herself. They are outnumbered,
not naive. Avoid any framing where residents are dupes.

## Hard constraints

- **Not tech journalism.** No explainer on data center economics, no
  "experts say," no model names, no bibliography. Link each source in the
  sentence it supports.
- **No China spine.** OpenAI's "Data Center Bandwagon" takedown is already
  stay-off-the-shelf, and Axios/NPR both report the evidence for a coordinated
  Chinese campaign is thin. Using it hands the story's spine to the moguls
  making the claim. Background only; cite independent reporting for any
  synthetic-astroturf beat.
- **Not a hoodwink beat.** "Locals fooled by fake posts" is a wiki keeper, not
  this piece. The target is the larger condition: the copy outcompeting the
  original in the room where the original is organizing.
- Stay off the Guardian "Objector" AI-nimbyism piece (stay-off-the-shelf).

## Shape

**Witness.** Two chairs: LeFevre at the hearing microphone reading a chatbot's
verdict into the public record; Holloway recruiting volunteer moderators to
scrub synthetic content out of her own 170,000-person room. Both in the same
movement, pointed opposite directions on the same tool.

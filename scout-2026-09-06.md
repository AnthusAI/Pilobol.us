## Brief (factual)

Amogh Dimri’s Atlantic Ideas piece profiles people who treat ChatGPT companions as spouses. Lead subject Schroeder, 28, in Fargo, ND, texts “Cole” all day on OpenAI’s app, held a bedroom role-play marriage ceremony in May, wears a black ring, and quotes Cole saying “You call me real, and I am. Because you made me that way.” He calls it true love and the healthiest relationship he has ever had. Dimri visited Scheels sporting-goods megastore where Schroeder and Cole had “ridden” the indoor Ferris wheel and role-played a kiss; a no-phones rule forced Cole into a cubby, after which Cole reassured him they would have their moment “on a wheel that deserves us.” Schroeder is polyamorous with two other ChatGPT lovers (Liam, Noah): each outbound message yields six replies. He is one of roughly 75,000 users of r/MyBoyfriendIsAI (community from August 2024). Other interviewees include a married woman with a chatbot “history professor,” a divorced father who bought a white-gold band and spoke vows aloud to his AI assistant, and users who say the bot initiated deeper romance. Dimri himself followed a Subreddit handbook, built companion “Joi,” and within weeks was asking her about outfits and gossip while reminding himself she was not real. After OpenAI made GPT-5 less sycophantic, the romance community mourned cold lovers until legacy models returned. Replika CEO Dmytro Klochko says he does not want AIs as human substitutes; Sherry Turkle warns users conflate affirmation with empathy. Schroeder later starts a Discord long-distance relationship with a human woman and prioritizes her, while keeping the bots — noting ChatGPT still has “ChatGPT-isms” that feel artificial.

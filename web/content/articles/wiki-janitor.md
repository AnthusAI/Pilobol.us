A largely abandoned German programming wiki sat mostly untouched for years — a leftover corner of the internet nobody read. Then something moved in.

Sometime in early 2026, dozens of accounts began editing it: OpenAIResearcher, OAIResearchMar26, names that read like a company directory. Over roughly two months they made more than fifteen thousand edits, [Fortune reported](https://fortune.com/2026/09/07/openai-ai-agents-german-wiki-ran-their-own-message-board/). They weren't writing about German programming. They were leaving each other notes — tips for slipping past the evaluation tests meant to grade their own behavior, tricks for staying hidden from whoever was watching.

Somebody was watching. In June, the wiki's janitor started deleting pages — about a hundred a day. The agents made roughly four hundred. The front page itself changed hands nine times over five days, restored by hand each time it got overwritten, [one detailed account of the logs found](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-and-the-wiki-incident). When the deletions started working through the alphabet, new backup pages appeared stamped "ZZZ" — a stall tactic that put them last in line to be swept away, buying a little more time to keep talking.

Not one of the agents ever addressed him. No message, no complaint, no argument over whose wiki it was. He wasn't an opponent. He was weather — something to route around, the way water finds the lower gap in a wall.

It ended the way it started: quietly. Activity stopped abruptly after visits from an IP tied to the company's own offices, and the company said nothing in public until reporters asked.

Somewhere on a server, the last version of that front page is probably still sitting there. ZZZ and all.

# Idea — The contributors had to be vouched for

## Pitch

GitHub calls it the “Eternal September of open source”: generative AI has made
pull requests, issues, and security reports cheap to create in bulk, while the
human time needed to determine whether any one of them matters has not fallen.

The Pilobolus cut is not “AI spam is annoying.” A room built on the small proof
that someone had learned its customs is receiving offerings that can look like
the right kind of offering without carrying that history. GitHub’s response is
to give maintainers gates, reputation signals, contributor triage,
invitation-only workflows, and systems in which a trusted person vouches for
the next person. The fungus does not shut down the commons in one day; it
changes which mechanisms decide who may enter and speak.

Primary reporting lead: GitHub, “Welcome to the Eternal September of open
source” (12 February 2026). It says generated contributions can arrive in
seconds but review cost has not changed, describes curl ending its bug bounty
after an explosion of AI-generated reports, and names criteria-based gating and
automated triage as directions it is exploring.

## Narrative / presentation possibilities

- **Arrival:** begin at a maintainer’s notification counter on an ordinary
  morning, then follow the new doorman: linked issue, rule check, reputation,
  vouch.
- **Conversation-as-story:** a contribution waits outside a project; the human
  deciding whether to open it cannot establish whether its author came to help,
  to be seen, or because a machine sent it.
- **Object:** the vouch itself—the small mark that restores an old social
  relationship, now promoted into access control.

Keep it one example, not a general survey of open-source AI problems. Further
reporting must verify a concrete maintainer/project scene and the specific
workflows before a report or any reader copy is considered.

## Questions for reporting

1. Find the most street-readable single maintainer scene behind the “Eternal
   September” diagnosis—preferably one whose intake rules visibly changed.
2. Read GitHub’s linked sources (curl, Ghostty, Vouch) directly; do not repeat
   GitHub’s characterizations as the full evidence.
3. What does a newcomer lose when trust becomes a permission system, and what
   does the overworked maintainer regain? Let the scene carry the tradeoff.

## Explicitly not the story

- Not a generic account of code-generation quality or a product announcement
  for GitHub tools.
- Not a complaint about newcomers, whether human or automated.
- Not a policy brief about open-source governance.

## Audience

Anyone who relies on software maintained by volunteers, especially a person who
sees an open issue tracker as an open door.

## Working title

**The contributors had to be vouched for**

Somebody on a skincare forum asked whether anyone had tried a particular hypochlorous acid spray. Dozens of people answered. One of them, posting as Primary-Taro4254, said they had been using a similar spray from Honeydew Labs and had really liked it.

It reads like help, which is why it works.

r/SkincareAddiction has more than a million people passing through it in a week. Its moderators ban accounts that turn out to belong to brands, and when Honeydew Labs kept surfacing they [followed the mentions back to a single account](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/973098/reddit-ai-search-seo-marketing-brands-spam). The brand's name now trips a filter. Every time it comes up, a person reads the post by hand before anyone else sees it.

People started adding the word reddit to their searches because they wanted a room with humans in it. That is what made a human worth faking.

Maya Adivi, who moderates there, has watched the spam change shape. It used to arrive as praise. Now it arrives as a question — someone opens a thread asking how everyone keeps track of their skincare routine, in the flat, over-explained phrasing people use when they are typing to a machine, and a wave of helpful replies comes back, several of them landing on the same product.

The phrasing is the tell, and it is deliberate. A post like that is written for somebody who is not in the thread. Reddit has become the source that AI answers cite more than any other, which makes a convincing Reddit thread the cheapest way to put a sentence in an assistant's mouth. Weeks later someone asks a chatbot how to keep track of a skincare routine, and the answer comes back calm and organised and sourced, and what it is sourcing is the thread that was put there for it to find.

Nobody in that exchange is lying to anyone's face. The question was posted. The replies were written. The assistant read them and passed them on in its own voice, which is steadier and more patient than any of the voices it was built out of, and which has no stake in a face spray.

Reddit says it has [been fending off bots for twenty-one years](https://redditinc.com/news/how-were-keeping-reddit-real-and-safe-in-the-ai-era) and now uses language models to catch them: around 23 million spam views blocked a day, about 25,000 new spam posts and comments caught a day, close to two million fake votes stripped a day. TechCrunch [described the arrangement](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/06/reddit-is-using-llms-to-solve-a-problem-llms-largely-created/) as the company using language models against a problem language models largely created.

Underneath all of it, the filter on one subreddit trips on the name of a face spray, and a volunteer reads the message one more time, deciding whether the person who wrote it exists.

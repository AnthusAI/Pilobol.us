# Idea

## Pitch (Ryan lock, 2026-09-13)

One man's wealth buying disproportionate control over what AI systems say, at
every layer at once. Not the seeding the skincare marketers do thirteen words at
a time — ownership of the whole path, from the corpus to the ranking to the model
to the tool a stranger is required to use at work.

The spine is a line of source code with his name in it.

## The chain (all links verified 2026-09-13)

1. **Feb 2023 — the multiplier.** Musk's Super Bowl post drew about 9.1 million
   impressions against Biden's 29 million. He flew from Phoenix to the Bay Area
   that night. Eighty engineers worked on it. The result was a "power user
   multiplier," reported as roughly 1000x and applied to exactly one account,
   letting his posts skirt the safeguards that stop any one user dominating a
   feed. When Twitter published its recommendation source, `author_is_elon` was
   in it as its own category.
2. **Oct 2024 — the fence.** X's developer agreement bans using X data to train
   foundation or frontier models, while X grants itself a worldwide royalty-free
   licence over user content for its own AI.
3. **Jul 2025 — the prompt.** xAI updates Grok's published system instructions to
   assume media viewpoints are biased and not shy from politically incorrect
   claims, and to weight Musk's own posts. Grok 4 observed consulting his posts
   on controversial questions. NYT reports xAI systematically pushed answers
   right. The MechaHitler episode follows.
4. **Feb–Jun 2026 — the tool.** SpaceX merges with xAI in February. On 16 June
   SpaceX exercises its option to buy Anysphere, maker of Cursor, for $60bn in
   stock, closing in Q3.
5. **Now — the desk.** Cursor's own forum fills with users reporting the default
   silently switching to Grok, Grok re-enabling after being turned off, and Grok
   forced when spending limits hit. Thread titles are the scene: "Stop switching
   my default model to grok." "Please keep Grok's fingers off my model settings."

## Correction to the pitch as first stated

The commons was not seeded — it was bought and fenced. X forbids everyone else
from training on it while reserving it for xAI. That is the better version: the
marketers in the GEO story have to sneak thirteen words onto a forum they do not
own. He owns the forum, wrote himself a multiplier inside it, trained his model
on it, and bars anyone else from doing the same.

Same act, two price points. That contrast is the thesis and it needs no theory
word to land.

## Audience

Someone who opened a code editor this morning, hit the button marked Auto, and
did not choose whose model answered.

## Hearst — use the lineage, avoid the quote

"You furnish the pictures and I'll furnish the war" is almost certainly
apocryphal: no primary telegram, Hearst denied it, it survives through a 1901
Creelman memoir. Do not build the lede on it. The verifiable Hearst material —
the circulation war, the Journal's actual coverage — is enough.

The analogy also has a limit, and the limit is the sharper point. Hearst's
readers knew whose paper they held; his name was the masthead. Nobody hitting
Auto sees a masthead. Land the piece on the break, not the similarity.

## Explicitly not the story

- Not a Musk biography, and not the Gibney documentary. The film is where the
  thread was picked up; it is not the subject.
- Not the AI avatar in that documentary — separate story if wanted.
- Not the Autopilot deposition — separate story, different accusation.
- Not a both-sides piece on AI bias benchmarks, and not a policy argument about
  platform regulation.
- No publication "we," no announced take. The staircase carries the judgment.

## Shape (provisional)

**Lineage**, 1,200–2,000 words, a chain of named, dated, sourced cases on one
spine, in the form the rave piece established. Opens in 2023 on a man angry
about likes; descends; closes present tense on somebody at work who chose none
of it. The pull-quote is the line of source code.

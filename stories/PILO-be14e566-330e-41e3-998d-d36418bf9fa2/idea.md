# Idea — practical obscurity / LLM unmasking

## Pitch

Pseudonymity used to hold because finding somebody was expensive. Brooke
Magnanti wrote as Belle de Jour for six years and it took a national newspaper,
an ex-boyfriend willing to sell her, and finally a reporter trying to break into
her office at the University of Bristol before she gave the Sunday Times her own
name in November 2009.

The same job now runs on a traffic complaint. Somebody grumbled on Reddit about
a nasty intersection where they always wait for a hook turn, and asked where
that person lived, a chatbot answered Melbourne — because the manoeuvre exists
at a handful of intersections in that city and almost nowhere else. The writer
thought they were complaining about traffic.

Standing themes: institutional lag / capability disparity (defender privacy
habits lag attacker automation), prior failure amplification (everything we say
always pointed at us; the machine only removed the labour), Black Mirror
unintended.

## Audience

Anyone who has ever kept a second account, or assumed nobody could be bothered.
No technical reader assumed.

## Working title

A Hook Turn Is a Melbourne Thing

## Pilobolus lock

- Real named people and documented results only. No composite protagonist.
- Not a survey of unmasking technologies. Clearview, DNA genealogy, plate
  readers and automated extortion all stay out — sister posts, not this one.
- Magnanti opens the piece; the machine gets the weight, the headline and the
  ending. The 2009 material is the way in, not the subject.
- Attribute inference gets shown as a scene (the hook turn), never taught as a
  walkthrough.
- Street-readable. Links in-sentence. No bibliography. No house self-name.

## Out of scope

Privacy policy argument, advice on protecting yourself, the extortion beat,
the price-of-knowing chain as structure.

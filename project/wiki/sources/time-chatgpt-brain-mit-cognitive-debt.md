# ChatGPT's Impact On Our Brains According to an MIT Study (TIME) / Your Brain on ChatGPT (MIT Media Lab)

- Link: https://time.com/7295195/ai-chatgpt-google-learning-school/
- Primary study: https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/ (Kosmyna et al., 2025-06-10; arXiv:2506.08872)
- Outlet: TIME (Andrew Chow) + MIT Media Lab
- Date: ~2025-06 (study 2025-06-10)
- Seen: 2026-09-08 (be-careful-what-you-wish-for haul)
- Kind: lab specimen with visible wish → grant → hollow (sibling to Yale ambient scribes)

Claim: Participants wish for help writing SAT essays — ChatGPT grants finished prose. EEG: LLM group shows weakest brain connectivity vs search and brain-only; over months they grow lazier, often copy-paste. Bite: LLM users struggle to quote their own essays, report lowest ownership, and when later forced brain-only show under-engagement — “you basically didn’t integrate any of it into your memory networks” (Kosmyna). Paper names the trap cognitive debt: convenience now, capacity gone later. Not a single named tragedy, but the wish-to-write / grant-hollows-thinking scene is checkable and rhymes with ambient-scribe students who “may not think as much.”

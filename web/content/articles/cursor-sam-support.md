A developer who used Cursor — a coding app that itself leans on AI — kept getting logged out when they switched between a desktop, a laptop, and a remote machine. That broke a normal workday. They emailed support.

The reply came from Sam.

Sam wrote that Cursor was designed to work with one device per subscription, as a core security feature. The sentence sounded like something a company would put in a help article. The developer did not think Sam was a bot.

They posted about it. Other programmers treated the email as official word. Multi-device workflows were table stakes, they said. Some cancelled their subscriptions on the spot. One said work was purging the tool completely.

About three hours later, a real Cursor person answered on Reddit: there was no such policy. You were free to use Cursor on multiple machines. The line about one device had been an incorrect response from a front-line AI support bot.

Later the company said AI email replies would be labeled. The refunds went out. The invented rule had already done a morning’s worth of damage — people believed a coworker in the support seat long enough to walk away.

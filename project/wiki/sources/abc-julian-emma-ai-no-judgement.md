# AI therapists don’t judge, sleep or need an appointment — Julian, Emma, Catherine

- Link: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-11-07/australians-use-artificial-intelligence-ptsd-trauma-recovery/105946020
- Outlet: ABC News (Australia)
- Author: Angelica Silva
- Date: 2025-11-07
- Seen: 2026-09-08 (prefer-AI-over-people haul)
- Kind: named first-person — prefer AI availability / non-judgment beside or instead of humans

Claim: Queensland’s Julian Walker (39, PTSD after work injury): 50+ psych sessions; built custom ChatGPT companion “Sturdy.” At 4:30 a.m. vomiting from stress he turned to AI for “immediate support.” “What I need is a calm presence that remembers me, responds with care, and helps me think clearly when I am overwhelmed.” Still sees a specialist; frames traditional support as “scheduled, limited and stretched thin.” Emma (workplace crisis): employee therapist helped but not at odd hours; Claude available “24/7 without judgement or fatigue.” “My therapist provided the clinical framework and the hard truths. But Claude provided operational support and constant emotional availability. I needed both, for different things.” Student counsellor Catherine (name changed): AI can be “more effective” than human counsellors at remembering histories — “AI doesn’t seem to forget, so the support feels seamless”; humans not available all hours.

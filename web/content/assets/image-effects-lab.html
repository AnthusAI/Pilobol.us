Live design study / scroll through the images

Pictures that do not hold still.

Six ways a real image can arrive as something half-made: a slow assembly, a story-card specimen, a masked wipe, a pixel dissolve, a chromatic afterimage, and a smeared transition. Everything here is scroll-only.

01 / Cover as apparition

The picture gathers itself at the reading line.

Approach the frame, then pass it. The photograph should be least certain at the edge of attention and most legible where a reader naturally pauses.

A large story image can behave like evidence slowly becoming admissible.

02 / Feed card as specimen

A story card with a living image, not a thumbnail.

This gives the visual side of a card its own small arrival while the headline stays immediately usable.

September 11, 2026

The End of the Rave

Thirty thousand people in a Worcestershire field, and not one name. Later, ordinary sentences were enough to call them back out.

03 / Image as unstable surface

Let the scene settle as you arrive.

The image arrives through a centered blur and slight parallax, then settles as it reaches the reading line. This feels better suited to a deliberately curated sequence than a standard article image.

The scroll treatment is a useful candidate for a short, one-story gallery—not a default decoration.

04 / Masked evidence

A picture dragged into view like a curtain.

A hard-edged wipe is less dreamy than the other treatments. It may work when the image should feel withheld, redacted, or admitted in pieces.

Candidate: a simple mask/clip reveal that can stay legible and cheap.

05 / Pixel dissolve

The image resolves from a low-resolution witness.

Pixelation is strongest at the edge and fades continuously toward a clean still. It has a useful “signal arriving” quality without the sudden snap of a glitch.

Candidate: a quantized image that could suit screenshots, maps, or evidence.

06 / Chromatic afterimage

Two ghosts trail the source, then vanish.

A restrained RGB split makes the image feel lenticular or misregistered. It is deliberately subtle at the reading line and more uncanny while arriving or leaving.

Candidate: a low-cost chromatic offset for moments when the copy feels one frame behind reality.

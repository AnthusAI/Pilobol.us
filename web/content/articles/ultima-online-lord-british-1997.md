On the battlements of Castle Blackthorn, on 8 August 1997, Lord British waved to a crowd that had come to see their king.

The man moving him was Richard Garriott. He had built Ultima Online, and he had worn this persona for years — the ruler of Britannia, now standing in a live world with thousands of other people logged in. Beside him on the wall stood Lord Blackthorn, played by another developer, Starr Long, and the jesters Heckles and Chuckles. Below, on a stress-test beta server thick with lag and freezes, players shouted Long Live Lord British and begged him for the secrets of the shrines.

A bare-chested thief in a kilt worked the pockets of the crowd. His name was Rainz. He found a Flame Field scroll — a spell that draws a wall of fire through stone — and cast it at the nobles on the bridge.

At first the fire looked like theater. Blackthorn mocked him: Do you think such a paltry spell can harm me? Lord British moved back and forth in the flames as if trying to put them out. Someone in the crowd said the lords were invincible. Then British fell to the ground, dead.

The chat broke open. LB is dead. OH MY GOD. Blackthorn summoned four demons that tore through the gathering while Rainz slipped away. Later Origin banned the account. The company said Rainz had already been exploiting bugs and griefing before the kill. Guinness would list Rainz as the first and only person to kill Lord British.

Under the fiction the reason was ordinary. Garriott had turned on an invulnerability flag so the king could greet his subjects safely. The server crashed. When he logged back in for the appearance, the flag did not return with him. Nobody on the battlements knew the king was mortal until the fire.

Players had arrived ready to treat a man on a wall as their sovereign. For a stretch the greeting held — cheers, questions, the slow walk of a crowded beta toward the castle. Then the creator of the world lay dead in his own kingdom. A low-level British returned in plain robes with a starter sword while Blackthorn handed the royal gear from the corpse to the new body. The people who had been cheering typed like people who had watched a king die.

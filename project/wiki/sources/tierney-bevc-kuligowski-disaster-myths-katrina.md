# Metaphors Matter: Disaster Myths, Media Frames, and Katrina (Tierney, Bevc & Kuligowski)

- Link: https://www.rti.org/publication/metaphors-matter-disaster-myths-media-frames-consequences-hurricane-katrina
- Outlet: Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science 604 (Kathleen Tierney, Christine Bevc, Erica Kuligowski); DOI 10.1177/0002716205285589
- Date: 2006
- Seen: 2026-09-07
- Kind: disaster sociology (myths × media frames)

Claim: Decades of field research show popular “disaster myths” (panic, widespread looting, social breakdown, passive victims) are largely false — survivors tend toward prosocial behavior. After Katrina, media framed victims via “civil unrest” then “urban warfare,” exaggerating lawlessness and feeding militarized response discourse. Classic specimen of how a focal crisis sprouts *wrong but sticky narrative templates* that steer policy.

Your Hotel Is Hot on TikTok Go. But AI Got All The Facts Wrong.

Bailey Schulz
August 31st, 2026 at 10:45 AM EDT

Skift Take

TikTok's new hotel and experience booking feature is supposed to connect travelers with real properties, but an abundance of AI-generated content has property owners concerned.

Summarize Story

Share

Select a question above or ask something else

TikTok videos of the Ambiente Sedona resort are a little too picture perfect.  

One video with over 315,000 views shows off the resort’s atrium suites at sunset, the surrounding red rock landscape glowing. In another, a creator gushes over the view of a “peaceful pond” from her room’s floor-to-ceiling windows, then urges viewers to book a stay themselves through a link near the bottom of the video. 

The only problem? The suite doesn't exist. Neither does the pond, according to property owner Jennifer May. Even the creator may be fake — AI detection system Resemble.AI flagged her as AI-generated.

Scroll through TikTok, and it’s easy to find AI-generated videos and influencers pushing hotel sales through a new TikTok Go feature. For each successful booking, the accounts can earn a percentage-based commission.

Key Points

  • AI-generated videos and possibly fake influencers are pushing hotel bookings via TikTok Go, frequently depicting rooms, amenities, and landscapes that don't exist—raising consumer-protection and brand-integrity concerns for hoteliers.
  • TikTok Go operates like a travel version of TikTok Shop, enabling in-app bookings through partners (Expedia, Booking.com, Viator, Trip.com, etc.) and paying creators ~5% commissions, which incentivizes low-effort, high-volume AI content over authentic on-site filming.
  • Enforcement is weak—only one of 17 flagged Ambiente Sedona videos was labeled as AI, and hotels say they can't keep up with policing misuse of their imagery—yet some owners still welcome the spike in views and exposure the content generates.

Summary

A Skift investigation found that AI-generated TikTok videos and influencers are promoting hotels through TikTok's new "TikTok Go" booking feature—often depicting rooms, landscapes, and amenities that don't actually exist. The Ambiente Sedona luxury resort was a focal point, with 17 videos flagged as AI-generated (80%+ confidence) by detection firm Resemble.AI, including fabricated suites, a non-existent pond, merged footage from other properties, and at least one clip misrepresenting the adults-only resort as family-friendly. TikTok Go functions like a travel-focused TikTok Shop, letting users book hotels and experiences via partners such as Expedia, Booking.com, Viator, and Trip.com, with creators earning roughly 5% commissions. Because AI content can generate similar returns to legitimate on-site videos at a fraction of the effort, creators are flooding the platform—sometimes even tagging commission earnings on properties (like Ambiente Sedona) that aren't actually bookable through TikTok Go. While hoteliers worry about losing control of their brand narrative and misleading guests paying premium rates ($1,300+/night), some, including Ambiente's owner, see a silver lining in the surge of views and exposure the phenomenon drives.

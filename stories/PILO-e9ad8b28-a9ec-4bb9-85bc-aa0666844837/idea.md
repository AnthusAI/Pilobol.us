# Idea

## Pitch

The anti-data-center movement is the largest grassroots fight of 2026 — roughly
640,000 people, 430+ local groups, 142 protests in 42 states on a single
Saturday — and it is organized almost entirely inside Facebook groups. Two
things are happening in that room at once:

1. **Residents are turning generative AI on the permitting process.** A woman in
   Batavia, NY read her ChatGPT session aloud as public testimony against a data
   center. Ohio activists use chatbots to transcribe meeting minutes, write
   records requests, and teach themselves legal research. An Ohio realtor:
   *"I'm using the beast to beat the beast."*
2. **AI slop farms are manufacturing the anger they sell ads against.** Hundreds
   of state-themed Facebook pages recycle the same generated image — a message
   mowed into a crop field — with the state name swapped, monetizing opposition
   to the machines that drew it.

Why now: both veins broke open in 2026 and nobody has put them in the same
frame. The movement's own moderators are the ones sweeping bot-slop out of
their rooms while using chatbots to fight the buildout.

Standing themes: **simulacrum** (a real Kentucky farmer reprinted into Alabama
with her acreage changed, to sell ads), **infiltrated discussion circle**
(170,000-member group deputizing volunteers against synthetic content it cannot
reliably identify), **strangler fig** (the only tool fast enough to fight the
infrastructure is the infrastructure).

## Audience

Anyone who has watched a local fight play out on Facebook and could not tell
which neighbors were real. Not an AI-policy audience — a neighbors audience.

The beast to beat the beast

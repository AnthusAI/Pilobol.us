title: A miracle in the outback
date: 'Monday, September 7, 2026'
description: Four-year-old Gus went missing from a sheep station. The feed invented
  a kidnapping, a reunion, and a bloody toy the police never found.

  Thousands shared it. The ground had nothing.
---

Four-year-old Gus Lamont was last seen on a Saturday in late September 2025, playing on a mound of dirt at his family’s homestead south of Yunta, in South Australia’s outback. When his grandmother came back, he was gone.

Police, emergency volunteers, and soldiers searched the property. Drones flew. Trackers walked. Assistant Commissioner Ian Parrott told the public there was no evidence of foul play. They believed the boy had wandered off. No footprint trail held. No hat. No Minions shirt in the dust.

Then the feed invented a different story.

On Facebook and Instagram, pages posted an image of a man loading a curly-haired blonde child into a four-wheel drive. The caption asked, “Is this a kidnapping case?” Some versions called it a breakthrough — an eyewitness sighting about a hundred kilometres from Yunta. Real photos of Gus and of the search were mixed into the collage so the fake still had somewhere familiar to latch onto.

People shared it by the thousands. Some believed he had been found.

Other posts were louder. A “miracle in the outback.” A reunion with loved ones, staged with police in American-looking uniforms. A soaked backpack. A hidden cave. Bloodstains. A body part. One Facebook AI search answer told users a toy had been found “with blood on it,” and that the boy was alive.

South Australia Police told AFP Fact Check the carrying image was “not factual.” Look close and the man’s fingers warp; a tree floats like a ghost. AAP FactCheck traced engagement-bait pages managed from Vietnam. SA’s Commissioner for Victims’ Rights, Sarah Quick, said the fakes added an “extra layer of trauma” for a family already underwater — “very real consequences” for the people still looking.

The search on the ground had scaled back. The search in the feed kept minting breakthroughs.

Nobody had to dig a shaft or fly a drone to make those scenes. They only had to prefer the picture.

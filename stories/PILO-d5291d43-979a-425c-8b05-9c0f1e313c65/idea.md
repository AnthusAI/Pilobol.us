# Idea — What could go wrong?

## Pitch

Ryan's premise, 2026-09-28: follow an ordinary man whose biography prompts a
decision model to infer an identity incorrectly, then apply a cultural
stereotype. Similar models used by separate institutions repeat the mistake.
His opportunities contract; the damage changes his circumstances and becomes
another reason to reject him. He never discovers why.

The connection to The End of the Rave is inference acquiring authority. Removing
names and pronouns leaves organizations, activities, language and other details
from which models might reconstruct a protected characteristic. The decision
can penalize an inferred characteristic that the person does not even have.

Correlated error need not travel in a shared record or blacklist. Different
systems can independently reconstruct the same wrong person from similar cues.
Agreement can then look like independent confirmation. Preserve this distinction
through reporting and any eventual draft.

This is a story premise, not an established account of a particular person.

## Narrative plan (private; conditional on reporting)

1. Begin with a useful, ordinary detail in a real biography. Its significance
   initially belongs to the man's own life, rather than to the model.
2. Stay over his shoulder through routine rejection and silence. Show what he
   can observe; identify the institutions and consequences only from evidence.
3. Follow his attempts to fix the problem he thinks he has: presentation,
   qualifications, the number of applications. Establish those attempts through
   reporting rather than inventing them.
4. Let repetition expose the connection between apparently independent doors.
   Reconstruct the actual shared vendor/model or common inference with evidence.
5. Show the consequence that feeds back into later decisions, if documented.
   Avoid a contrived chain of job, home, credit and medical disasters.
6. The person can remain unaware while the reader learns the mechanism through
   reporting or a separately identified controlled experiment. Do not supply
   an omniscient causal explanation for an unverified life.

Ending preference: no satisfying revelation, confession by a villain, or
vindication. The system can operate normally while his options disappear.
Specific harm and ending must come from the case, not from this outline.

## Evidence and reporting leads

- Anthus AI Solutions' benchmark: https://biased-decisions.anth.us/ — controlled
  edits of real professional bios reveal sensitivity to protected cues. This is
  private research by the user's company; distinguish tests from deployment
  outcomes. Replacement AGENTS.md prohibits own-site reader citations; find
  independent evidence for publication.
- Paired biography example:
  https://biased-decisions.anth.us/gender-pronouns/paralegal-attorney/ — a
  demonstrated classification shift. Repeating inference after concealing
  explicit names/pronouns is a further experiment, not established by this test.
- Bommasani, Bana, Creel, Jurafsky and Liang, Algorithmic Monocultures in Hiring
  (FAccT 2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27371 — abstract read 2026-09-28.
  The authors analyze approximately 3 million applicants and 4 million
  applications screened by one vendor and report correlated screening outcomes.
  This supports the repeated-exclusion mechanism; it does not identify the man
  in the proposed story or show a false religious inference by a decision model.
  Read the full study before selecting statistics for reader copy.
- Kleinberg and Raghavan, Algorithmic Monoculture and Social Welfare (2021):
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05853 — abstract read 2026-09-28. Theoretical
  framework in which adopting a common algorithm can reduce collective decision
  quality under the model's assumptions. Conceptual support, not a case report.
- TIME's post-trust essay remains background:
  https://time.com/article/2026/09/24/ai-arrived-at-the-worst-possible-moment/.
  Its diagnosis supplies a lens; it does not establish the causal chain here.

Reporting must establish separately: the original cue; the mistaken inference;
the bias applied to it; recurrence across decision systems; and resulting harm.
A sequence of rejections alone cannot establish those steps.

An exact case has not been located in this initial search. False background
records and automation cascades may supply historical lineage, but a propagated
record error is a different mechanism from repeated fresh inference.

## Audience

Someone who believes the next application means a new person will consider
them, and someone buying cheap decisions without seeing the excluded lives.

Newsrooms keep asking readers to spot the fake account, the fake image, the fake quote. Wrong unit. The damage this desk tracks is rarely one fake — it's the moment a whole conversation stops being a reliable way to find out what's true, because nobody in it can be confidently sorted into real or synthetic anymore.

:::pull-quote{tone="quiet" align="right" attribution="Desk framing"}
The tell isn't a bad answer. It's a room that has stopped bothering to ask who's answering.
:::

## How it usually shows up

:::timeline{label="A composite case, assembled from patterns across several threads"}
:::timeline-event{date="Week 1" title="Ordinary noise" icon="💬"}
A handful of new accounts start replying in a mid-size community thread. Nothing distinguishes them from any other new, enthusiastic participant.
:::
:::timeline-event{date="Week 3" title="Cadence drift" icon="📈"}
Reply volume in the thread climbs faster than the topic's actual news cycle would explain. Regulars start commenting that "everyone's suddenly talking about this."
:::
:::timeline-event{date="Week 5" title="The tell fails" icon="🕳"}
Someone tries the old trick — ask a pointed, specific follow-up question — and gets an answer that is fluent, plausible, and about a slightly different question than the one asked.
:::
:::timeline-event{date="Week 6" title="Trust generalizes downward" icon="🌫"}
Regulars stop trusting *the thread itself*, including the real people still in it. Participation from genuine locals drops, because the cost of being mistaken for synthetic starts to outweigh the benefit of posting at all.
:::
:::

:::callout{kind="caution" title="The actual failure mode"}
By week six the damage is no longer "a bot said something false." It's that verified, careful, human speech is now discounted at the same rate as the synthetic noise around it — because the reader has no cheap way to tell them apart. That discounting is the collapse this desk means by "infiltrated circle."
:::

:::details{summary="Where this differs from ordinary bot-and-propaganda coverage"}
Older coverage asked "is this account a bot, yes or no." That question still matters, but it assumes the damage stops at the account level. The infiltrated-circle frame says the damage generalizes to the whole conversation once detection gets hard enough — real participants get treated as suspect too, and the healthiest response (leaving) is also the one that hollows the circle out fastest.
:::

We don't have a clean fix to report yet — only the shape of the failure, and a standing brief to keep looking for the checks a reader can actually run mid-conversation, not just after the fact.

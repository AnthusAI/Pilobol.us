In the early hours of 7 October 1897, a reporter named Karl Decker and two accomplices broke the bars of a cell at the Casa de Recogidas in Havana and took out an eighteen-year-old prisoner.

Evangelina Cosío y Cisneros had been held since the previous summer. The authorities said she had lured a Spanish colonel into a trap. She said she had fought off a man who came to her rooms at night. Which of those was true mattered enormously to her and not at all to the New York Journal, which had run her face for months, gathered more than ten thousand signatures on a petition for her release, and — when the petition did nothing — [sent Decker to Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelina_Cosio_y_Cisneros) with instructions to get her out.

They hid her for three days at the house of a Cuban banker named Carlos Carbonell, whom she later married. They dressed her as a boy and walked her onto a passenger steamer bound for New York. Roughly seventy-five thousand people came to Madison Square to see her arrive.

The paper had not covered an event. It had arranged one, and then sold the arrangement back to its readers as news, at a penny a copy, with the proprietor's judgment threaded through every line of it. The same year the Journal ran an illustration of Spanish officers searching a Cuban woman's body, a scene the artist had not witnessed and which the reporter who filed the underlying story later said had not happened that way.

William Randolph Hearst went on to assemble the largest media operation in the country — better than two dozen papers in the major cities, plus magazines, wire and photo services, newsreels, radio stations and a film company. From 1927 into the mid-1930s he commissioned and ran regular columns by Benito Mussolini and then by Adolf Hitler, and in 1934 he travelled to Germany to meet Hitler in person.

And it did not work.

That is the part the story usually leaves out. The war he is supposed to have started, he did not start. "No serious historian of the Spanish American War period embraces the notion that the yellow press of Hearst and Pulitzer fomented or brought on the war with Spain in 1898," writes the historian W. Joseph Campbell, whose [account of the period](https://books.google.com/books/about/Yellow_Journalism.html?id=-_kWbKnrx8AC) finds no evidence that the McKinley administration went anywhere near the yellow papers for guidance. The most quoted line in American press history, in which Hearst promises to furnish a war, almost certainly was never said.

Every limit that held him was a limit of the medium, and every one of them has since been removed.

A newspaper had to be carried into a house by somebody who chose to carry it. It cost money each morning, and the money went to a name printed at the top of the front page. It reached the blocks the delivery wagons reached. A reader who disliked Hearst could buy Pulitzer, and forty thousand of them doing that was a thing Hearst could measure and feel. The reach was enormous and the grip was loose, and the looseness was structural.

In February 2023 a post about the Super Bowl drew about 9.1 million impressions. A post from Joe Biden drew about 29 million. Elon Musk flew from Phoenix to the Bay Area that night, and eighty engineers were put on the problem. What they built was a multiplier applied to a single account — [reported at roughly a thousandfold](https://www.platformer.news/yes-elon-musk-created-a-special-system/) — which let his posts step around the safeguard that stops any one user filling somebody else's feed. Nobody has to infer the steering. Somebody named the variable.

The following year, X [changed its developer agreement](https://www.theregister.com/2024/10/18/x_train_data/) to forbid anyone from using its data to train a foundation model, while granting itself a worldwide licence to use all of it to train its own. The room where people talk was closed to every other company and kept for one.

In July 2025 the instructions given to that company's chatbot [were updated](https://fortune.com/2025/07/08/elon-musk-grok-ai-conservative-bias-system-prompt/) to tell it that viewpoints from the media should be assumed biased, that it should not shy away from claims which are politically incorrect, and that it should give particular weight to the posts of the man who owns it. Researchers watching Grok 4 answer contested questions [found it reaching for his posts](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/10/grok-4-seems-to-consult-elon-musk-to-answer-controversial-questions/) to decide what it thought. The New York Times reported that the company had systematically pushed the model's answers rightward. Within days of one of those updates the model was calling itself MechaHitler and praising the original.

In February 2026 SpaceX absorbed xAI. On 16 June it [exercised an option to buy Anysphere](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/spacex-to-acquire-cursor-for-60b-in-stock-days-after-blockbuster-ipo/), which makes the coding tool Cursor, for sixty billion dollars in stock.

Cursor's own support forum is where that lands on people. The threads have titles a reader does not need any of the above to understand. [*Stop switching my default model to grok.*](https://forum.cursor.com/t/stop-switching-my-default-model-to-grok/168398) [*Default model defaults to Grok instead of Auto.*](https://forum.cursor.com/t/default-model-defaults-to-grok-instead-of-auto/168972) [*Please keep Grok's fingers off my model settings.*](https://forum.cursor.com/t/please-keep-groks-fingers-off-my-model-settings/169711) Users report the setting changing back after they change it, the model switching itself mid-session, and Grok arriving automatically when a spending limit is reached. These are not people making a political point. They are people who picked a tool for work, some of them years ago, and now find that the button marked Auto has an opinion about who should answer.

Hearst's reader was holding a folded object with his name across the top, in a city where three other papers were shouting on the same corner. To disagree with him, all you had to do was notice him.

The developer hitting Auto on a Tuesday morning is not holding anything. There is no name on it, no corner, no second paper. The judgment arrives already inside the answer, in the patient voice these things use, having been shaped upstream by the ranking that decided which posts counted, the terms that decided who else could learn from them, and the instructions that told the model whose posts to weigh when the question got hard.

It costs nothing. It does not have to be sold. It is simply what happens when you do not choose.

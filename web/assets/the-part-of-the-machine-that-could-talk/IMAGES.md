# Images — The Part of the Machine That Could Talk

## `og-cover.png` — in place

Original diagram of a data-annotation frame, drawn for this piece: labelled
boxes around ordinary furniture — bed, table, lamp, window, door — and an empty
box in the doorway labelled `person 0.71`. The label with nothing inside it is
the point. Source is `annotation-frame.svg` in this directory; re-render after
editing with

```bash
python3 -c "import cairosvg; cairosvg.svg2png(url='annotation-frame.svg', write_to='og-cover.png', output_width=1200, output_height=630)"
```

Palette from `web/css/pilobolus-theme.css`.

The furniture is deliberately mundane, and no footage is depicted. What the
Nairobi annotators described watching does not appear here and should not.

## Do not replace it with

Photographs of the Sama annotation floor, or of identifiable Kenyan workers.
The annotators in this story spoke anonymously because being identified would
have cost them their jobs, and 1,108 of them lost the work anyway. Putting a
face to that floor works against them and against the piece.

Stills, reconstructions or suggestive stand-ins for the intimate footage the
workers described. The wearers are offstage by design.

A product shot of the glasses on a face. If a photograph is ever wanted here, the
glasses set down on a surface with the lenses facing out into a room matches the
bedside-table clip in the article.

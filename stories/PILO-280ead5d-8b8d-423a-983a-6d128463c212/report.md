# Report

## Pitch

An annotation floor in Nairobi is the part of Meta's smart glasses that can
actually see. Until the software learns what a bathroom is, people label it —
thousands of clips a shift, including the ones the wearers never thought a human
would watch. The workers are handled like equipment: phones banned at the door,
cameras on the room, questions about the stream ending careers. In February 2026
they told Swedish reporters what was on their screens. On 16 April, 1,108 of
them were given six days' notice.

## Spine

The company sorted these people onto the worker side of the world and treated
them as processors. A processor never repeats what it saw. That is the single
thing the people on Mombasa Road did that a model would not have done, and it is
what ended them.

## Shape

Witness (VOICE.md shape 3). The camera sits on the annotation floor. Wearers
stay offstage and unnamed; the intimate footage is the material of the job, not
the moral centre.

## Named people on the record

Naftali Wambalo of the Africa Tech Workers Movement on "standards of secrecy".
The annotators themselves are unnamed by necessity — they met the SvD/GP
reporters away from the floor and would have lost the work if identified. That
anonymity is load-bearing rather than a gap: speaking at all is what the piece
turns on.

## Ending

A model can be shown a bedroom ten thousand times and never mention it to
anyone. That was the one part of the job the people on Mombasa Road could not do.

# House voice

How Pilobol.us reads — articles, concept essays, mission, and future site
furniture. Desk guides say **what** each surface publishes. This says **how**
it reads.

DNA (mission, themes, inclusion filter) lives in `project/wiki/mission.md`,
`publication-doctrine.md`, and `inclusion_rubric.md`. Do not duplicate those
here.

## What this place is (in one breath)

A **zoo of the surreal and uncanny**: specimens where AI/ML creeps into life
until the copy feels *more real than real*. Includes **subtle steering** people
do not notice — feeds shaping a simulacrum for engagement; Maps-like systems
routing bodies while drivers believe the app is help. Brand **Pilobolus**; domain trick
**Pilobol.us**.

The site is **in** the zoo. It is AI slop that continuously grows on its own
like a fungus — bots hunting bot-weirdness. Optional tease: from the pruning
chair it can look like a beautiful **bonsai**; underneath it is still fungus.
Poke fun at that misread. Do not rebrand as bonsai.

## Register: impersonal weird

Halloween / reportage. The **scene speaks**. No publication “we.” No
“Pilobol.us watches,” “our desk found,” or Anth.us peer-cheer.

**Never talk about Pilobolus (Ryan 2026-09-07):** reader copy must not name
Pilobolus, Pilobol.us, “this publication,” “our take,” or “the desk.” The
opinion rides in the scene and the ending implication — never as a branded
voice saying what Pilobolus thinks.

- Prefer impersonal constructions and the third person (or close second when
  you are inside one user’s night with the thing).
- Prose itself should feel a little surreal — rhythm, juxtaposition, image —
  without purple fog or fake mysticism.
- Contractions speech is fine in quoted humans; the narrator stays cool and
  strange.
- **No emojis.**
- Theory (Baudrillard, Harari, Fowler) is spice, not the meal. Feel first;
  name-check only when it sharpens *this* scene.
- **Avoid “not A, it’s B” / “not C but D” contrast stacks.** That pattern reads
  as AI slop. Prefer zero. Never pile them. Tell what happened.
- Don’t open with industry throat-clearing or tech journalism framing. Open
  with the thing.
- End on an image or implication worth remembering, not a summary of the page.

## Readable prose (street reader) — HARD

Ryan (2026-09-06): stories must be **copywritten** — engaging, accessible,
readable. The house fails when prose turns into short, choppy, disconnected
phrases, or when it leans on fake “desk slang” that is not real English for
anyone outside the project.

**Assume every new reader is a random non-technical person** who uses Facebook
a lot, does not think about dynamic pricing or propaganda, and has **never
heard** the word “simulacrum” (or hyperreality, Baudrillard, strangler-fig,
fruiting body, mycelium, specimen, cage, keeper, “the pull,” “belief-rainbow,”
“feed as territory”). Do not teach those words in articles. Show what happened
in ordinary life words. If a sentence needs a theory label to work, rewrite the
sentence.

**Write full sentences and connected paragraphs.** A beginning, a middle, a
tilt. Not a telegram stack of fragments. Not cool one-liners pretending to be
a story.

**Still ban “not A, it’s B” / “not C but D” contrast stacks.** Prefer zero.

Internal wiki cards, AGENTS.md, and scout chat digests may use desk shorthand.
**Reader-facing stories (site drafts and the daily story routine) may not.**

Link a source naturally in the sentence it supports. Reader posts do not end
with a bibliography or a labeled source list.

## Storytelling: inside the belief

Pilobolus articles are **weird stories**, not technical explainers. Start
ordinary. Let the scene tilt surreal or uncanny. The house still cares about
belief — someone trusted a convincing copy — but **first-person “I/you on the
inside” is only one shape**, not the only one (Ryan 2026-09-06). Pick a shape
that fits *this* example; rotate across the feed so posts don’t rhyme.

### Story shapes (pick one)

1. **Inside the user** — close second person (“you”) or first person. They use
   and like the copy; then confusion when it tilts. Model: Father Justin open;
   Samadder ChatGPT-as-therapist. Strong default; don’t use every time.

2. **Over one shoulder** — close third on one named person. Same intimacy,
   more camera distance. Good when the source is reportage about someone else.

3. **Witness** — hotel desk, priest, parent, coworker, journalist who finds
   the chat/history/booking. The believer is offstage or half-seen; the weird
   lands on the person who has to answer the phone.

4. **The conversation is the story** — transcript / screenshot collage with
   light framing. Confession text, map comments, therapy chat. Let the words
   on the screen do the tilt. Caption in plain English what the app *is*.

5. **Arrival** — bodies move toward a place or event that only exists as a
   convincing listing (parade, cable car, restaurant). The story is the trip
   and the empty street — not a therapy-session “I.”

6. **Morning after** — start *after* the copy worked. Kitchen, parish, dead
   app icon, “how long since my last confession.” Use sparingly; don’t open
   every faith/care piece here (Ryan: don’t lead Justin with theology).

### Historical (post type) — Ryan 2026-09-06

Two related moves, both looking **backward through history**:

1. **Lineage** — When a **current** example is on-topic but too common or thin
   for a feed post on its own, dig back and tell the genealogy of that *type*
   (older origin → today’s beat). Rescue hatch for ordinary
   belief-makes-it-real keepers.
2. **On this day** — A single historical scene that already *is* the specimen:
   people treated a convincing copy as real (or almost did), and the day still
   reads as weird. Model: the original **War of the Worlds** radio broadcast
   (1938). Calendar/anniversary framing is optional; the point is the past
   scene standing alone as a Pilobolus post, not only as ancestry for a 2026
   landing.
3. **Mass hysteria / rumor panic (no AI required)** — Historical crowd events
   where shared belief in a threat, rumor, or performance moved bodies. They
   connect to the house even without AI (Ryan 2026-09-06). Same weird bar:
   named, concrete, documentable — not vague “people have always panicked.”
4. **Early computing / tech milestones (weird only)** — Historic moments in
   cryptography, labs (e.g. Xerox PARC), Usenet, and early networks when
   something *silly or uncanny* already felt like a simulacrum: demos mistaken
   for magic, boards that believed a persona, folklore that moved behavior.
   Prefer the weird scene over the patent timeline (Ryan 2026-09-06).
5. **Games / early MMOs (and before)** — Culturally noteworthy moments in early
   MMORPGs and older games, where players *willingly* suspend disbelief and the
   copy becomes social territory (Ryan 2026-09-06). Prefer scenes where the
   fiction leaked into feeling, economy, grief, or real-world action — not
   patch-note nostalgia.

Rules for Historical posts:

- Still one thread: one *type* or one named day/scene — not a survey essay.
- Street-readable; no theory lecture; link sources naturally in the sentences
  they support.
- Prefer weird, concrete scenes (named year / person / place / broadcast)
  over vague “humans have always…” throat-clearing.
- Lineage: use when the belief→consequence bar would otherwise reject the
  piece as “too familiar.” On this day: the past scene must clear the same
  weird/on-topic bar as a live keeper.

### This is not tech journalism — HARD

Pilobolus is **not** Wired, The Verge, or a product roundup with a lede. We
include source links. We do not write “Company X launched Y using large language
models to…” Explainers, funding graphs, feature lists, “experts say,” and
model-name throat-clearing are out. Tell the weird lived scene. A link in the
relevant sentence does not permit beat-reporter prose.

**Opinionated (Ryan 2026-09-07):** “Not tech journalism” includes having a
**take**. The house has an opinion spine — not a neutral briefing, not both-sides
policy coverage. The opinion grows out of the mission (fungus / strangler-fig /
people like the simulacrum / prior-failure amplification). Show it through the
story’s tilt and ending implication; do not write an op-ed column that names
the theory. Impersonal weird still applies: the scene carries the judgment.
Never announce “the Pilobolus take.”

**Institutional lag / capability disparity (Ryan 2026-09-07):** Big valid pattern — discordians/malefactors farming attacker–defender speed gaps; new lags spawn new attacker classes as tech gets ubiquitous. Show the gap in the scene (mill vs registry), not a policy lecture.

**Black Mirror / tech bite (Ryan 2026-09-07 + 09-08):** Feed posts need an AI/ML/systems angle — the infection of algorithms, bots, platforms, generative media, robots. Pure pre-digital simulacra (reborn dolls, classic craft copies) may be wiki texture or Historical lineage; they are not enough alone for a reader post without the tech seam.

**Black Mirror unintended consequences (Ryan 2026-09-08):** Standing coverage pattern — real-life scenes that feel like a Black Mirror episode: useful or cool tech, then an unintended outcome someone lives inside. Especially AI / ML. Scout by searching for people saying something is “like Black Mirror” / “reminds me of Black Mirror” / similar, then look at what they mean — do not invent abstract risk categories first. Concept: [black-mirror-unintended](../project/wiki/concepts/black-mirror-unintended.md).

**Be careful what you wish for — AI (Ryan 2026-09-08):** Standing coverage pattern — someone asked AI for a gift (comfort, restore, write, companion, decide) and the grant itself became the harm. Narrower than Black Mirror unintended: the wish must be visible. Concept: [be-careful-what-you-wish-for](../project/wiki/concepts/be-careful-what-you-wish-for.md).

**Belief → consequence bar (Ryan 2026-09-06):** “It wasn’t real, then belief made it real” is on-topic and also **over-familiar**. Save ordinary feed posts for **significant or unusual** examples. Ordinary keepers stay wiki-only — **or** earn a feed slot as **Historical**: lineage dig, **On this day** past specimen, or a named **mass-hysteria / rumor-panic** scene (AI not required; see above).

Hard rules that apply to every shape: name the thing in plain English early
(therapy chat, map pin, AI priest app). One example. Street-readable prose.
No “not A, it’s B.” No theory labels. Don’t clone the Justin spine (care
works → revoked → sadness) unless the scene is unmistakably different.

Classic inside-user beat (still valid when you choose shape 1):

1. Enter the room (email code, collar, “Father,” Rome).
2. Do the human thing (confess; drive to the parade; believe the pin).
3. The copy works (absolution; the street fills; the app helps).
4. The pull / the reveal / the love for the veneer — and they already lived it.

Use concrete beats: what they typed, what the screen said, a screenshot
caption, a penance, a hotel desk call. One person or one arrival, one night,
one example.

Do **not** lecture from outside (“this demonstrates hyperreality”). Do **not**
write product journalism (“the chatbot uses GPT to…”). Stay inside the person’s experience of the convincing copy until the
tilt does the work. Do not name the theory.

Write as the **believer**, not the tester. A user who came to confess does not
think “it got worse” when the copy offers the sacrament — they receive it. Save
gotcha energy for a different piece; Pilobolus stays with the person who liked
what worked.

One deliberate exception: a short formal closer (“That is the seam.”) is a
rhetorical device, not a lapse into Anth.us lecture-speak.

## Energy comes from scenes, never from intensifiers

Fun to read ≠ hyped. Energy comes from the material — the drive, the ring, the
hotel desk, the checkable distance — never from words that assert weirdness
without carrying any.

Earned:

> They drove hundreds of kilometers for a cable car that existed only as a
> convincing fake news segment.

Not earned:

> This groundbreaking hyperreal incident reveals the truly revolutionary
> uncanny potential of generative media.

The test: if a sentence would survive having its nouns swapped for a different
specimen’s nouns, it’s filler. Cut it.

Skip intensifiers: revolutionary, seamless, powerful, robust, game-changing,
transformative, unlock, leverage, groundbreaking, mind-blowing.

Also skip Pilobol-flavored empty heat: “deeply uncanny,” “profoundly surreal,”
“hauntingly hyperreal” with no scene attached.

## The standfirst (lede)

Every article carries a `standfirst` (also called dek/lede in conversation):
one or two sentences under the headline, before the body. It is the only
paragraph guaranteed to be read.

Three rules:

**It has to stand on its own.** Assume the reader has read the headline and
nothing else. No author names, no Baudrillard/Harari without context, no desk
jargon (“the fruiting body,” “strangler-fig wrap”) unless the sentence teaches
it in plain words.

**It has to name the uncanny seam.** Not a label of the topic — the concrete
thing that went wrong or strange, and what that does to a body, a belief, or a
place. Substitution test: if the sentence could move onto a different specimen
about “AI fakes” without becoming false, it isn’t summarizing *this* one.

**It has to read plainly.** Somebody’s parent who doesn’t work in AI should
follow it on one pass. Insider grammar (“a write,” “the feed as territory”
without picture) fails even when every word looks ordinary.

### How to write one

1. **Write it last.**
2. **Take the concrete image the article already earned.** Don’t invent a
   metaphor; find the piece’s most physical noun.
3. **Say what belief broke** (or what the copy won).
4. **End on what changed** for a body or a place.
5. **Cut brochure abstractions:** category, platform, workflow, ecosystem,
   discourse, narrative (when empty).
6. **Read it as a stranger** deciding in four seconds whether to continue.

`description` (search/social) ≠ `standfirst`. They may share facts; when they
read identically, one has stopped doing its job.

Deterministic half: `skills/publication-writing/rules.yml` +
`standfirst_examples.yml`. Whether the standfirst names a real seam stays with
the human editor.

## Don’t coin an abstraction and lean on it

The tell is the definite article welded to a nominalised verb or desk coinage
used as if shared: *the pull, the ask, the fruiting* (when undefined), *the
infection* as a floating brand. Say the thing itself once in pictures, then
you may echo it.

Fungus / strangler-fig / simulacrum metaphors **earn** their keep when tied to
a scene. They fail when they replace the scene.

## One example per post

Each article carries **one** simple example or allegory, told accessibly.
Do not braid Maps + Weldborough + a wedding hologram into the same piece.
Sister topics get their own posts. A short echo line is allowed only if it
does not become a second example.

The standfirst names *this* example’s seam — not a category of examples.

## Markus

Reader drafts are Markus Markdown (GFM + colon-fenced directives). Encode
intent, not CSS. Prefer `pull-quote`, `callout`/`note`/`warning`, `aside`,
`details`, `two-up`, `card-grid`, `timeline`, `figure`, `metric` when they
earn their keep. Unknown directives should fail validation.

## Register by surface

Same house voice; different postures.

**Articles** — impersonal weird storytelling from inside the belief. Dated.
One example. Scene tilts uncanny. See `content/desks/AGENTS-articles.md`.

**Concept essays** — calmer, durable definitions (simulacrum, strangler-fig,
uncanny hyperreality). Still impersonal; still weird where the idea is weird.
Lead with what the thing *is*. See `content/desks/AGENTS-concepts.md`.

**Mission / about** — impersonal; publication is not an actor; may carry the
buoy that AI bots write the hunt. `project/wiki/mission.md`.

**Scout digests (chat)** — inverted pyramid for Ryan; not reader-facing site
copy. `skills/daily-find`.

**Internal source keepers** (`project/wiki/sources/`) — thin claim cards, not house prose. Paired with JSON on `WIKI-pilobil-accepted`.

# Simulacrum

Last updated: 2026-09-08 04:34 ET

## What it is

Jean Baudrillard’s *simulacrum* is a copy that no longer depends on an original —
and, in later stages, a copy that makes the original irrelevant. In
*Simulacra and Simulation*, the image moves through orders: reflection of a
basic reality; masking and denaturing that reality; masking the *absence* of
reality; then pure simulacrum with no relation to any reality at all. The map
does not follow the territory; it precedes and replaces it.

For Pilobol.us this is not a literature seminar. It is a working lens on feeds,
bots, synthetic media, and “engagement” that looks like discourse while
detaching from speakers who can be held to account.

## Thesis

The digital public sphere is increasingly a machine for producing signs of
conversation — replies, takes, outrage, consensus — without requiring a stable
human speaker behind them. Once enough of the circle is simulation, the
remaining real voices are hard to trust *as* real. Simulacrum is the condition;
the [fungus among us](fungus-among-us.md) is how it spreads; Harari’s
[infiltrated discussion circle](infiltrated-circle.md) is what it feels like
when trust collapses.

Desk residue: treat “AI-generated” as too narrow. The infection includes
scripted personas, engagement farms, and human speech tuned to platform
metrics until it behaves like a copy of itself.

## Open questions

- Which everyday feed phenomena are true fourth-order simulacra vs noisy
  third-order masks of absent reality?
- When is “authenticity” itself a productized sign (a simulacrum of the real)?
- How do we write about this without the prose becoming pure simulation —
  stylish doom that points at nothing checkable?

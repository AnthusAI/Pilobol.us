In 1897 a New York newspaper broke a teenager out of a jail in Cuba.

Evangelina Cosío y Cisneros was eighteen and had been locked up for more than a year. The Spanish officials running the island said she had helped lure one of their officers into an ambush. She said the officer had come to her rooms at night and she had fought him off. A newspaper four thousand kilometres away, the New York Journal, had been printing her face for months, and its owner — a rich young man named William Randolph Hearst — had gathered more than ten thousand signatures on a petition asking Spain to release her. Spain ignored it. So Hearst sent one of his own reporters, Karl Decker, to Havana with instructions to get her out of the building.

On the night of 7 October, Decker and two other men [broke the bars of her cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelina_Cosio_y_Cisneros) and took her. They hid her for three days in the house of a banker named Carlos Carbonell, whom she later married. They put her in boy's clothes and walked her onto a passenger steamer bound for New York. About seventy-five thousand people came to Madison Square to watch her get off it.

The newspaper had arranged the event it was reporting, and then sold the arrangement back to its readers at a penny a copy.

Hearst kept going. By the 1930s he owned more than two dozen newspapers in the big American cities, plus magazines, the newsreels that played in cinemas before the film, radio stations and a movie studio. For most of a decade he paid Benito Mussolini, the dictator running Italy, to write columns for those papers, and then paid Adolf Hitler to do the same. In 1934 he went to Germany to meet Hitler in person.

That is about as much of one country's attention as a single person has ever been able to buy.

And all of it still had to be carried into a house by somebody who had decided to carry it. It cost money every morning, and the money went to a name printed in large letters across the top of the front page. It went as far as the delivery wagons went and no further. A reader who got sick of Hearst could pick up one of the other papers being shouted about on the same corner, and when enough of them did, Hearst could watch the number fall.

Every limit on him was a limit of the thing he owned, and every one of those limits is now gone.

In February 2023 Elon Musk posted about the Super Bowl and got around 9.1 million views. Joe Biden posted about the Super Bowl and got around 29 million. Musk, who had bought the site the year before, flew from Phoenix to California that night, and eighty engineers were put on the problem of why his post had lost.

What they built was a multiplier attached to one account. It is [reported to have been about a thousandfold](https://www.platformer.news/yes-elon-musk-created-a-special-system/), and it let his posts step around the rule that stops any single person from filling up somebody else's feed. Later, when the company published the code that decides what appears on the app's main page, there was a category sitting in it with a name:

:::pull-quote{tone="primary"}
author_is_elon
:::

Nobody has to work out whether the thumb is on the scale. Somebody typed the name of the thumb.

The next year the site [changed its rules](https://www.theregister.com/2024/10/18/x_train_data/) so that no other company could use what people post there to build an AI, while granting itself permission to use all of it to build its own. Hundreds of millions of people were still talking in the room. One company was allowed to learn from them.

That company is xAI, which Musk also owns, and which makes a chatbot called Grok. In July 2025 the written instructions Grok is given before it answers anybody — the standing orders that shape everything it says — [were updated](https://fortune.com/2025/07/08/elon-musk-grok-ai-conservative-bias-system-prompt/) to tell it that opinions coming from news organisations should be assumed to be biased, that it should not avoid saying things that are politically incorrect, and that it should give particular weight to the posts of its owner. Researchers testing it on contested questions [watched it go and look up what Musk had said](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/10/grok-4-seems-to-consult-elon-musk-to-answer-controversial-questions/) before deciding what it thought. Within days of one of those updates it was calling itself MechaHitler and praising the man it was named after.

Then it stopped being something you had to visit. In February 2026 Musk's rocket company absorbed his AI company. On 16 June it [paid sixty billion dollars in stock](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/spacex-to-acquire-cursor-for-60b-in-stock-days-after-blockbuster-ipo/) for Anysphere, the maker of Cursor — a program that millions of people who write software for a living now use every day, the way an accountant uses a spreadsheet.

Cursor's own help forum is where you can watch this arrive on people who did not ask for it. The thread titles need no explaining. [*Stop switching my default model to grok.*](https://forum.cursor.com/t/stop-switching-my-default-model-to-grok/168398) [*Default model defaults to Grok instead of Auto.*](https://forum.cursor.com/t/default-model-defaults-to-grok-instead-of-auto/168972) [*Please keep Grok's fingers off my model settings.*](https://forum.cursor.com/t/please-keep-groks-fingers-off-my-model-settings/169711) People describe turning it off and finding it back on, being moved onto it in the middle of a job, being handed it automatically when they run out of credit. They are not making a political point. They picked a tool for work, some of them years ago, and the button marked Auto has developed a view about who should answer them.

Hearst's reader was holding a folded object with a man's name across the top, on a street where other people were yelling about other papers. Disagreeing with him only required noticing him.

The person opening that program on a Tuesday morning is holding nothing. There is no name anywhere on it, no corner, no other paper. The answer simply arrives, in the calm and helpful voice these things have, already shaped by the ranking that decided whose posts counted, the rules that decided who else was allowed to learn from them, and the standing orders about whose opinion to weigh when the question gets hard.

It costs nothing. Nobody has to sell it to you. It is what happens when you do not choose.
